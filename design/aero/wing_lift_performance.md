## 5. What the numbers mean

- **Hand launch:** the throw must give more than the stall speed with the tail down-load (about 9.2 m/s in Bengaluru and 8.8 m/s in Chennai). Wind adds to the airspeed: launch into the 4–6 m/s afternoon wind.
- **Bengaluru is the more difficult case**, because its air is about 10 % less dense. If the aircraft launches well in the December tests, it also launches in Chennai.
- **Cruise:** at 13 m/s the loaded wing needs CL ≈ 0.64 (Bengaluru) and 0.58 (Chennai). This is in the low-drag range of the SD7062 (Cd ≈ 0.012–0.013 from CL 0.2 to 0.7). After the drop, the CL is low (≈ 0.2): the aircraft climbs or must fly slower.
- **Structure:** below about 21 m/s the wing stalls before it reaches load factor 6, so a gust or a pull-up at normal flight speeds cannot overload it (spar margin ≈ 4 at that load, D-004).
- **Largest uncertainty: the wing CLmax.** If it is 1.20 instead of 1.35, every stall speed increases by about 6 %. Measure it with a throw test of a loaded dummy (brief Q6), then set `CLmax_wing` in `design/calc/wing_lift.py` and run it again.
- **Cross-check:** a nonlinear lifting-line analysis with the SD7062 polars (`design/aero/wing_lift_performance.py` → `wing_lift_performance.txt`, separate session) gives a 3D CLmax of about 1.41–1.45 at Re 120k–180k, and 1.21–1.31 if the 2D CLmax is 11 % lower. Its loaded stall speed is 8.7 m/s (Bengaluru) and 8.35 m/s (Chennai). The value CLmax = 1.35 used here is between those cases.
- These are wing-only values for steady flight. They do not include fuselage lift, ground effect, or the propeller slipstream.
