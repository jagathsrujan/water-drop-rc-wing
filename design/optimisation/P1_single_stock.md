# P1 — Single balsa stock study (rev B, Fusion model)

Study only: no CAD or part-data change. Date 2026-11-26. Basis: the rev B Fusion part data (`design/cad_fusion_revB/scripts/`,
`check/bodies.json`) and cited material data ([references.md](references.md)). Design rule from the user: **generic
locally bought balsa**. Strength is taken at a lower-medium density (0.12 g/cm³) and mass at plain medium
(0.14 g/cm³). Hand-sorted light sheets and premium grades are not counted on; better wood only adds margin.

## 1. Recommendation

**Buy one balsa stock: 1.5 mm medium, 100 × 1000 mm, 18 sheets** (15 % spare included). Every balsa part comes
from it: 1.5 mm parts stay one layer, 2–3 mm parts become 2 cross-grain layers, the LE and TE are laminated and
sanded, and the triangle longerons become an L of two 1.5 × 6 strips. Thicker ply parts become 3–4 layers of
cross-laminated balsa (formers, bulkhead, saddles, trays).

| | Today (as modelled) | Today, generic medium balsa | **1.5 mm single stock** | 2 mm | 1 mm | 3 mm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Empty mass | 721.0 g | 737.5 g | **721.1 g** (716–732 with glue 20–80 g/m²) | 765.8 g | 756.4 g | 856.0 g |
| Score (996 × 100 / empty) | 138.1 | 135.1 | **138.1** | 130.1 | 131.7 | 116.4 |
| Balsa to buy (100 × 1000) | 17 sheets in 6 types + 9 strip sizes | same | **18 sheets, 1 type** | 17 | 29 | 15 |
| Laminated parts | 0 | 0 | 58 | 46 | 139 | 24 |
| Lamination glue *(estimate 40 g/m²)* | 0 | 0 | 10.2 g | 7.5 g | 33.0 g | 3.6 g |
| Feasible | yes | yes | **yes** | yes | yes (D-box 2 × 1.0 laminated on the curve) | **no** (3 mm D-box will not wrap the LE) |

Why 1.5 mm:
- It is the only candidate that keeps the empty mass (721 g, score 138.1) **while dropping the light and hard
  grades**. Today's design at generic medium density would be 737.5 g. The 16.5 g that the grade change costs is
  paid back by replacing lite-ply with cross-laminated balsa (−18.9 g on the lite-ply parts, −3.9 g on the
  bulkhead).
- 1.5 mm is today's most-used thickness (D-box, sheeting, floor, outer sides, webs, false-spar keys), so most parts
  do not change at all.
- 2 mm makes the 1.5 mm parts (D-box, sheeting, floor, sides) thicker: +45 g. 1 mm needs 139 laminated parts and
  33 g of glue. 3 mm cannot make the D-box.

**Ply** (section 5): there are two options for the ply line of the BOM. The user chooses at the gate.

| Option | Ply to buy | Empty / score |
|---|---|---|
| **A — simplest BOM** | **1 sheet of birch ply 1.5 mm, 300 × 300**: grip zone, skids, flap stop and horns in 1.5; firewall, stub doublers and tube rings as 2 × 1.5 laminated; front stop as 2 × 1.5 birch | 728.1 g / 136.8 |
| B — lightest | birch 1.0, 1.5 and 3 mm (1 sheet each) + 1 sheet lite-ply 3 mm for the front stop only | 721.1 g / 138.1 |

My recommendation is **A**. Wood then means two items, one balsa stock and one birch-ply stock, against 21 wood line
items today. A costs 7 g (1.3 points). Most of that is the front stop (+4.1 g), which P2 re-designs anyway (see
section 6). The density of the balsa sheets you buy matters about five times more than this choice: ±0.02 g/cm³ is
±29 g (section 4).

## 3. Design basis (cited; see references.md)

| Value (MPa unless stated) | Balsa, design (ρ 0.12) | How |
|---|---|---|
| E along grain | 2,550 (mean) | R1 Table 5-5a 3,400 × 0.12/0.16 |
| Bending strength along grain | **11.9** | 21.6 × 0.75 × (1 − 1.645 × 0.16) [R1 Tables 5-5a, 5-6] |
| Bending across grain | 0.48 | 0.04 × along (R1 Hankinson Q/P lower bound) |
| Compression ∥ | 7.9 | 14.9 × 0.75 × (1 − 1.645 × 0.18) |
| Shear ∥ | 1.21 | 2.1 × 0.75 × (1 − 1.645 × 0.14) |
| Compression ⊥ (proportional limit) | 0.14 *(estimate)* | R1 Table 5-11a hardwood function at G 0.12, 5th percentile; extrapolated |
| E_T / E_L | 0.015 | R1 Table 5-1 |

- **Linear density scaling** within a species: R1 p. 5-26 and R2.
- **5th percentile** = mean × (1 − 1.645 CoV), with the CoV from R1 Table 5-6. Stiffness is taken at the mean.
- **Lite-ply and birch ply** are modelled as veneers of eastern cottonwood and paper birch (R1 Table 5-3a) at
  their 5th percentile. The lay-up is an *estimate* (3 mm lite-ply = 3 × 1.0, birch 1.0 / 1.5 = 3 veneers,
  birch 3 mm = 5).
- **Carbon strip:** R3 lower bounds, tensile 400 and compressive 200 MPa.
- **Loads:** D-064 (pod) and D-004 (wing), with ultimate = 1.5 × limit.

The old `strength_estimate.m` used uncited *interp.* values. Two of them were unconservative:
- **Cross-laminated 2 × 1.5 balsa in bending:** the old script allowed 0.5 × the along-grain strength. A layered
  section gives **0.27** (3.19 / 11.94): the cross layer adds almost no stiffness, so the along-grain layer
  bends about its own centre.
- **Balsa compression across the grain:** the old script used 0.6 MPa. The cited estimate is **0.14 MPa**
  (proportional limit, 5th percentile).

Assumptions of the mass model ([`p1_stock.py`](p1_stock.py); all inputs at the top of the file):
- Every balsa part is at 0.14 g/cm³.
- Lamination glue is 40 g/m² dry per interface *(estimate; range 20–80 shown)*.
- Laser nesting is 55 % efficient. Today's nesting is 39 %, spread over 11 thickness and grade types; one stock
  nests better. *(Estimate; 45 % → 21 sheets, 65 % → 17.)*
- Strips use 85 % of the sheet *(estimate)*, and 15 % spare is added.
- The pod's 10 % assembly-glue rule (from `mass_summary.py`) follows the pod's wood mass.

**Strips from the same sheet.** All strip parts are ≤ 984 mm long, so they fit along a 1000 mm sheet. The LE,
spar-cap balsa, D-box and centre sheeting run the full span. They are cut by hand with a balsa stripper or a steel
rule, not by the laser: the laser cuts 100 × 300 sheets. Laminating thin strips to a long block is a normal
modelling step, but straightness needs a flat board and weights. The **LE block** (4 layers, 984 mm) and the **TE
stacks** are the most work. Alternatives for P2: wrap the D-box round a small 2 × 1.5 LE, and use two 1.5 mm
skins over a spacer for the TE.

**Density is the big lever.** With the 1.5 mm schedule, the empty mass depends on the density of the sheets bought:

| Sheet density | 0.12 | 0.14 (design) | 0.16 | 0.18 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Empty mass / score | 692 g / 143.9 | 721 g / 138.1 | 750 g / 132.8 | 779 g / 127.8 |

Weigh every sheet when you buy (a 100 × 1000 × 1.5 sheet at 0.14 g/cm³ weighs 21 g). Use the lightest sheets for
the D-box, sheeting and tail surfaces, and the heaviest for formers, the bulkhead and spar caps. Sorting needs no
separate grade, only a kitchen scale.

## 6. Findings for P2 (they apply whatever the stock)

1. **Front stop (FS):** with cited values, the spoke check of `strength_estimate.m` fails in the weak grain
   direction. It does so for today's lite-ply too (margin 0.16; 2.2 along the face grain). It used to pass because
   the old allowable was an uncited 25 MPa for any direction. The four spokes should run at ±45° to the face grain,
   or the FS should be a full plate with windows. **FEA in P2.**
2. **Floor crushing under the saddles** at the 10 g landing: 0.34 MPa against 0.14 (margin 0.41; the old check
   with 0.6 MPa passed). A balsa or birch bearing pad under each saddle foot, or a wider foot, is needed. This is a
   P2 change.
3. **The isotropic plate formula is too crude for orthotropic laminates** (the grip zone fails it even as built).
   The grip-zone FEA in P2 should use the orthotropic constants of R1 Tables 5-1 and 5-2.
4. **Ribs 2 → 1.5 mm** save 3.4 g. Check rib crushing at the cap notches and handling in P2.
5. **The inner side layer 1.0 → 1.5** costs 5.3 g: the hex lightening (rev B saved ≈ 7 g) more than pays it back.
6. **The tips and tip caps** (9.7 g in 2 × 1.5 medium) are an easy lightening target.
7. **Wing:** the carbon cap compression margin is 2.0 (D-004 load, R3 lower bound) and the root web shear margin
   is 1.04 with one 1.5 mm web (2.1 with two). Neither depends on the stock.

## 7. Risks

- **Lamination glue mass is the main unknown** (10 g at 40 g/m²; 5–20 g over the range). Weigh a 2 × 1.5 test
  laminate and use thin spread aliphatic glue, rolled and weighted.
- **Local sheet density:** the score moves about 2.6 points per 0.01 g/cm³.
- **Warping:** cross-grain 2-layer laminates are balanced in-plane but not through the thickness. Glue them flat
  under weights and let them cure fully before laser cutting.
- **Slot widths change** (formers 4.5 mm, bulkhead 6 mm). P3 must update the slots, tabs, the jig fits and
  `check_joints.py`.
- The ply proxies (cottonwood, paper birch) are estimates of the real lite-ply and aircraft ply. They are
  conservative for aircraft birch (Betula pendula plies are usually stronger), but not verified.

## 8. India

100 × 1000 mm balsa sheets are sold in India (for example JustKraft BWS series, R5). The thicknesses offered and
the grades were not verified online. Ask the shop for 1.5 mm and weigh the sheets: medium is about 21 g per
100 × 1000 × 1.5 sheet.

## 9. Decision (gate P1)

**2026-11-26, the user: "Yes, 1.5 + A".** Single balsa stock **1.5 mm medium** (generic local grade; strength at
ρ 0.12, mass at ρ 0.14). Ply **option A**: one 300 × 300 sheet of **1.5 mm birch ply**. The grip-zone inners go
1.0 → 1.5. The firewall, stub doublers and tube rings become 2 × 1.5 laminated birch, and the front stop becomes
2 × 1.5 birch (P2 re-designs it). No lite-ply. Expected empty mass 728.1 g, score 136.8, before the P2 lightening.
