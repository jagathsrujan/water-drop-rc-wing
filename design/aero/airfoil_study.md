# Airfoil comparison study (analysis only)

Date: 2026-11-21. Status: numbers and observations. This file does not choose the final airfoil.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This study compares 8 airfoils for a small RC wing. The aircraft has 1 m span maximum, 0.20 to 0.32 m chord, 1.45 to 1.70 kg loaded mass, and 9 to 16 m/s flight speed. Air density is 1.05 kg/m3. Kinematic viscosity is 1.75e-5 m2/s. The Reynolds number range is 120k to 250k. The decision belongs to the main session and the user.

Candidates: Clark Y, NACA 4412, NACA 6412, E423, S1223, SD7062, MH 114, CH10. No candidate failed. No candidate was replaced. The CH10 file is the smoothed version `ch10sm.dat`.

## 2. Method

- Solver: NeuralFoil 0.3.3, model size `xlarge`. NeuralFoil is a neural-network surrogate trained on XFOIL data. XFOIL itself was not available.
- Cases: Re = 120k, 180k, 250k. Ncrit = 9 (clean field) and Ncrit = 7 (rough field or 3D-print surface). 48 polars in total.
- Angle of attack: the run uses -8 to 24 deg in 0.5 deg steps. This range contains the requested range of -4 to 18 deg. The extra range makes sure that low-CL points of highly cambered sections and the CLmax point are inside the grid. Plots show -4 to 18 deg only.
- Coordinates: UIUC database files (Clark Y, E423, S1223, SD7062, MH 114, CH10). The script reads only numbers from these files. NACA 4412 and 6412 are generated with the standard equation (sharp trailing edge, 100 points on each side). Files are in `airfoil_coords/`.
- Moment: Cm is about the quarter-chord point. The sign is negative for nose-down.
- Interpolation: Cd, L/D and Cm at a given CL use the pre-stall branch only (from -8 deg up to the CLmax point). A value shows "n/a" if the CL target is outside this branch.
- Stall sharpness: CL(at CLmax) minus CL(2 deg later). A larger number means a sharper stall.
- Checks: no NaN row occurs in any polar. In every case the CLmax point is inside the grid, but see section 4 for E423 at Re 120k.
- Re-run: `python airfoil_study.py airfoil_coords .` from this folder, with a Python that has numpy, matplotlib and neuralfoil installed.

## 3. Limits of the solver

- NeuralFoil is an approximation of XFOIL. XFOIL is itself an approximation of the real flow. A wind-tunnel result at Re below 250k can differ from both.
- CLmax and the post-stall shape are approximate. Treat CLmax differences below about 0.1 as not significant.
- Laminar separation bubbles control the drag at this Re. The surrogate smooths them. Cd values in the bubble region are uncertain.
- NeuralFoil gives an `analysis_confidence` value for each point (0 to 1). Low values (below 0.5) show that the case is outside the well-trained region. Table H and Table G list this value. Use the numbers with low confidence only as a trend.
- The model does not include the 3D wing effects, tip stall, the wing-body interference, film wrinkles, the turbulence of the propeller wash or real surface error. Real flight CLmax of a finite wing is lower than the 2D value.
- The Ncrit = 7 case is a simple model of a rough surface. It does not model real trip tape, a wavy film or layer lines.

### 4.1 Reading notes for the tables
- Table A: TE thickness of the NACA sections is 0 (the equation closes the trailing edge). Clark Y has 0.12 % c (0.32 mm at 270 mm chord). The concavity value is the largest distance of the lower surface above the straight line from the leading edge to the lower trailing-edge point.
- Alpha at CL 0.8 (Re 180k, Ncrit 9): Clark Y 3.6, NACA 4412 2.9, NACA 6412 1.1, SD7062 3.2, MH 114 -0.3, E423 -2.5, S1223 -2.2, CH10 -2.3 deg.
- "n/a" for alpha0 means that CL = 0 is not inside the pre-stall branch above -8 deg (E423, CH10, and MH 114 at some cases). The alpha0 values of the cambered sections are low-confidence. For example MH 114 gives -4.6 deg at Re 120k and -7.9 deg at Re 180k. Do not use them for design.
- Cd at CL 0.6 of E423, S1223 and CH10 (0.046 to 0.051) is a low-confidence value (confidence 0.30 to 0.74, Table H). The sections are at -4 deg or lower. Do not use it for design.
- E423 at Re 120k, Ncrit 9: the polar is not reliable. Cd is 0.08 to 0.10 for 8 to 14 deg with NeuralFoil confidence 0.33 to 0.40. The CL curve has a long plateau from 17 to 23 deg (CL 1.85 to 1.89). The table value (1.89 at 22 deg) is the highest point of this plateau. A first local peak of 1.86 occurs at 18 deg. CH10 at Re 120k shows a similar but smaller problem (Cd 0.10 to 0.12 for 8 to 13 deg).
- Table G "min confidence" covers alpha -4 deg up to the CLmax point. Low values for E423, S1223, CH10 and MH 114 come from the low-alpha and bubble regions. Clark Y, NACA 4412 and SD7062 have confidence of 0.95 or more in the whole range.
- The CL-alpha slope of S1223 near zero lift is irregular. The curve bends at CL 0.8 to 1.0 (see `airfoil_polars.png`). This is a surrogate feature.


## 5. Observations

Main case: Re 180k, Ncrit 9. The cruise lift coefficient of the aircraft is about 0.66 to 1.06 at 12 m/s (see `logs/wing_log.md`). Thus CL 0.6 to 1.2 is the useful range.

### 5.1 CLmax and stall
- S1223 has the highest CLmax: 2.25 at 12.0 deg. CH10 (2.03) and E423 (2.02) follow. MH 114 gives 1.77.
- NACA 6412 and SD7062 give 1.58. Clark Y (1.38) and NACA 4412 (1.41) are lowest.
- Ncrit 7 lowers CLmax by 0.01 to 0.07. The largest drops are E423 (-0.07), S1223 (-0.06) and NACA 4412 (-0.06).
- Re has little effect on Clark Y, NACA 4412, NACA 6412, SD7062, MH 114 and S1223 (change below 0.06 from 120k to 250k).
- E423 and CH10 lose 0.13 and 0.18 of CLmax from 180k to 120k (Ncrit 9). The E423 polar at Re 120k is unreliable (section 4.1).
- Stall sharpness (CL drop in 2 deg after CLmax, Re 180k Ncrit 9): MH 114 0.008, SD7062 0.013, E423 0.027, Clark Y 0.036, CH10 0.054, NACA 4412 0.059, S1223 0.072, NACA 6412 0.073. Small values mean a gentle stall. S1223 and NACA 6412 have the sharpest CL loss.
- The NACA 4412 CL-alpha curve has a plateau at CL 1.29 from 9 to 11 deg. It rises again to 1.41 at 16 deg. The 16 deg peak is probably a surrogate feature. Use about 1.3 as a cautious value for this section.
- Stall alpha: NACA 6412 stalls at 11 deg and S1223 at 12 deg. SD7062 stalls at 14.5 deg and MH 114 at 12.5 deg.

### 5.2 Drag at cruise CL
- At CL 0.8 (Re 180k, Ncrit 9), Cd is 0.0120 (Clark Y), 0.0122 (NACA 4412), 0.0122 (NACA 6412), 0.0132 (SD7062) and 0.0148 (MH 114). The first three are equal within the solver error.
- E423, S1223 and CH10 have Cd of 0.0317, 0.0330 and 0.0375 at CL 0.8. This is 2.6 to 3.1 times the Clark Y value.
- The cause is the angle of attack. CL 0.8 occurs at about -2.2 to -2.5 deg for these sections. At this angle, the surrogate predicts flow separation on the concave lower surface. NeuralFoil confidence is 0.29 to 0.70 here (Table H). Treat these three Cd values as a warning, not as an exact number.
- The high drag decreases with Re. At Re 250k, Cd at CL 0.8 is 0.0183 (E423), 0.0270 (S1223) and 0.0330 (CH10).
- The high-camber sections recover at higher CL. At CL 1.0, Cd is 0.0198 (E423), 0.0200 (S1223) and 0.0249 (CH10), against 0.0140 for Clark Y and 0.0138 for NACA 6412.
- MH 114 is the best of the cambered sections for CL 1.0 to 1.2 (Cd 0.0140 and 0.0148). NACA 6412 follows (0.0138 and 0.0158).
- Clark Y and SD7062 are best for CL 0.6 (Cd 0.0113 and 0.0120). Their drag grows fast above CL 1.0 (Cd 0.0180 at CL 1.2 for both).
- Ncrit 7 changes Cd at CL 0.8 by less than 0.001 for the low-camber sections. For E423, S1223 and CH10 it lowers Cd (for example E423 from 0.0317 to 0.0199 at Re 180k). This is probably a surrogate effect: earlier transition on a rough surface removes the predicted separation. Do not read it as a real gain.
- L/D maximum: MH 114 84.7 at CL 1.41, NACA 6412 77.0 at CL 1.47, NACA 4412 75.0 at CL 1.18, E423 72.2, Clark Y 71.5 at CL 0.98, S1223 68.9, SD7062 66.4 at CL 1.09, CH10 60.6.
- Maximum CL^1.5/Cd: MH 114 103.1, E423 94.9, NACA 6412 94.3, S1223 92.4, NACA 4412 82.6, CH10 81.3, SD7062 75.4, Clark Y 73.5. These maxima occur at CL 1.2 to 2.2. Most of these CL values are above the cruise CL.

### 5.3 Pitching moment
Cm at CL 0.8 (about c/4), from least to most nose-down: SD7062 -0.080, Clark Y -0.082, NACA 4412 -0.101, NACA 6412 -0.145, MH 114 -0.189, E423 -0.231, S1223 -0.235, CH10 -0.246.
The three high-camber sections have a Cm about 2.8 times the Clark Y value. The tail must give a larger download. This increases trim drag and tail size.

### 5.4 Ncrit effect
Ncrit 7 gives a lower CLmax in all 8 sections (0.01 to 0.07). It changes the maximum L/D by up to 7 (E423 72.2 to 79.0, again a surrogate effect on the bubble). Rough surface and the 3D-print layer lines should be modelled with Ncrit 7 or lower.

## 6. Buildability notes (geometry only)

Thickness values are for a 270 mm chord. "t90" is the thickness at 90 % chord. A thin aft section is fragile and hard to cut with a hot wire.

| Airfoil | Max thickness (mm) | t90 (mm) | Lower surface | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clark Y | 31.6 | 7.5 | Flat from about 30 % chord to the trailing edge | Balsa: ribs and sheeting lie flat on a building board. Film follows a straight line. Foam: easy hot-wire cut and strong aft section. Print: flat underside prints on the bed without support. TE is 0.12 % c (0.3 mm): sand or add a TE strip. |
| NACA 4412 | 32.4 | 7.4 | Slightly convex at the front, nearly flat aft | Same as Clark Y. Sharp TE needs a balsa or carbon TE strip. Rib template is simple to compute. |
| NACA 6412 | 32.5 | 7.5 | Slightly concave aft (0.9 % c = 2.4 mm at 69 % chord) | Balsa: the lower edge of the rib is nearly flat, so a flat board works with a small shim. Film bridges the 2.4 mm hollow without a problem. Foam and print: no special problem. |
| E423 | 33.8 | 7.2 | Deep concave (4.8 % c = 12.9 mm at 54 % chord) | Balsa: needs a jig or a spar notch, and a ribs-and-lower-spar layout. Film can sag between ribs. Use closer rib spacing. Foam: hot wire cuts the hollow, but the core leaves a thin concave aft part. Print: concave underside needs support or must print in a different direction. |
| S1223 | 32.8 | 3.0 | Deep concave (6.0 % c = 16.2 mm at 69 % chord) with a cusped aft end | Hardest. The aft section is a thin wedge (t80 = 2.1 % c = 5.7 mm, t90 = 3.0 mm). Balsa ribs are weak at the aft end. Foam core aft section breaks easily. Print: thin aft wall needs a nozzle of 0.4 mm or less and a stiff material. Film gives a cusp that sags. |
| SD7062 | 37.7 | 5.0 | Nearly flat (0.2 % c deep at 92 % chord) | Easy lower surface, like Clark Y. The section is 14 % thick, so a deep spar is possible. The tail section is thinner (t95 = 0.8 % c = 2.1 mm). |
| MH 114 | 35.3 | 5.8 | Concave aft (2.0 % c = 5.3 mm at 78 % chord) | Medium. Balsa needs rib shims and film that bridges 5 mm. Foam cut is feasible. Print: moderate overhang on the underside. |
| CH10 | 34.7 | 5.6 | Deep concave (4.9 % c = 13.3 mm at 61 % chord) | Like E423 but the aft section is thinner (t90 = 5.6 mm). Same jig and film-sag issues. TE is 0.01 % c, so it is sharp. |

Note: for hot-wire foam, a concave lower surface also means a larger material loss in the core and a possible wire-lag error at the front of the section. For 3D print, the layer-line direction affects the surface roughness (Ncrit near 7 or lower).
